What the agents do.

An example week in an office of about 12 people. This is the shape of the work. It is not a client, and it is not a result we are claiming.

  1. Intake reply

    A new inquiry gets a same-day reply that asks for the facts the firm needs, and books a time if the matter fits.

    Never. It does not decide whether to take the matter, and it does not quote a fee.

    A person approves the first reply, and every reply after that until the firm says the pattern is right.

  2. Document chase

    The agent notices what was asked for and not received, and sends the reminder on the day you set.

    Never. It does not read the document for legal meaning, and it does not file it to the court.

    A person sees the list of what is still missing.

  3. Deadline reminders

    Dates already on the firm’s calendar are repeated to the person who owns them, early enough to act.

    Never. It does not create a legal deadline, and it does not move one.

    The lawyer who owns the matter is the one reminded. The agent does not replace the docket.

  4. Review replies

    A public review gets a draft response in the firm’s voice, waiting for a person.

    Never. It does not post the reply itself.

    Someone at the firm posts it, or tells the engineer to change the draft.

  5. Follow-ups

    After a consult or a quiet file, the agent drafts the next note and queues it.

    Never. It does not give legal advice in that note.

    A person sends it. The portal shows what went out and what is waiting.

Confidentiality and access.

A firm has to be able to write this down. Many use ABA Formal Opinion 512, including Rule 1.6 on confidentiality and Rule 5.3 on supervising a nonlawyer who touches the work. This is the posture, before anything is connected.

  • Client files stay in the systems you already use. We do not keep a second copy.
  • Your information is not used to train a model. Where a step needs a model, we name the model and the vendor first, under terms that exclude training.
  • Access is the least the chore needs. It is written down. You can revoke it.
  • A confidentiality agreement is signed before anything is connected.
  • You can stop the agents. Stopping is the kill switch, and it is yours.
  • The portal shows what ran, what is waiting for a person, and what was blocked.
  • We do not have a SOC 2 report. If your vendor file needs one, we will say that in writing, with who can see what.

We will answer the firm’s vendor questions in writing before the work starts. This page is that posture. It is not the agreement.

Questions.

Why law firms first?

The chores are the same in a lot of owner-run offices: intake, documents, reminders, follow-ups. Law firms already have to think about confidentiality, so the access rules get written down properly. The next offices are dental practices and med spas, real estate offices, and CPA firms.

Keep it. The agents work with the system you have. Studio does not replace the practice software, and that subscription stays yours.

What happens when something breaks?

Agents miss steps, and offices change. You write it in the portal, or you say it on the monthly call. Fixing that is the monthly work. The reply time is agreed before the work starts. This page does not promise that nothing will break.

How long is this, and how do we stop?

The setup is once. The month is month to month after the day-30 checkpoint. Stop with 30 days’ written notice. The agents stop, the access is revoked, and you get a written list of what was connected and how it worked.

What is included in the $5,000?

Learning how this office moves work, and setting up agents for the chores you name. Not a generic install. The $1,000 a month is the engineer managing those agents as the office changes. Timing of the first chores is discussed on the call. The outline is week 1 to learn, weeks 2 to 4 to set up, day 30 to decide.

Can the staff see what the agents did?

Yes. The portal is the record: what ran, what is waiting for approval, what was blocked, and what you asked to change. You invite the people who should see it.

Who owns what gets built?

You own your files, your accounts, and the description of how your office works. The agents run on that. What happens to a specific workflow if you leave is written into the agreement, including the handover list.

How do we pay?

After the call, if we are going ahead, you are invited into the portal. You pay the $5,000 and the monthly charge there, by card, through Stripe. Invoices and the way to stop the monthly charge are in the same place. Paying is not available on this page before that conversation.
